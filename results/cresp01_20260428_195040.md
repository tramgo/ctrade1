## Cadence Sweep Follow-Up

Fresh follow-up result:

- `signal_baseline_portfolio_rank_60m_long_only_cadence_sweep` completed
- objective was exactly to find where the weekly long-only winner turns negative as the hold period is shortened by more frequent rebalancing

### Main result

The wrapper does not fail because turnover suddenly blows up.

It fails because:

- average turnover per rebalance stays broadly similar across cadences
- but the holding period gets shorter
- so the gross move captured per rebalance shrinks faster than the cost burden shrinks

In plain language:

- we are paying roughly similar trading friction each time we refresh the book
- when we refresh too often, the selected names do not have enough time to move far enough in our favor
- the edge gets consumed by cost before it compounds

### What eats profitability

1. Cost amortization failure:
   - for `E1006 top3`, turnover is `1.7402` at weekly cadence and still `1.7500` at daily cadence
   - that means the main problem is not "too much extra turnover"
   - the problem is "same order of turnover, less time for the selected stocks to pay for it"

2. Shorter hold means smaller realized edge per rebalance:
   - `E1006 top3` falls from `0.0041071` at `every_5` to `0.0026840` at `every_4`, then `0.0010802` at `every_3`, `0.0004904` at `every_2`, and finally `-0.0004715` at `every_1`
   - this is the clearest evidence that the selection edge is real but needs multi-day holding to monetize

3. Wider books dilute the edge sooner:
   - `top_k = 3` survives down to `every_2`
   - `top_k = 5` is already economically broken by `every_3` and fails benchmark comparison even at `every_2`
   - inference: adding names improves diversification, but it dilutes the strongest signal names too early when cadence is faster

4. Benchmark gap closes before sign flips:
   - `E1006 top5 every_2` is still slightly positive at `0.0000875`
   - but it is already below the incumbent benchmark return `0.0001012555869542`
   - so practical failure starts before raw negativity in some variants

5. This is still not a concentration artifact:
   - top contributor share remains moderate in the winning region
   - for `E1006`, it is `0.2205` at `top3 every_5`, `0.1060` at `top3 every_4`, `0.1353` at `top3 every_3`, `0.0969` at `top3 every_2`
   - the strategy is not surviving purely because of one runaway stock

### Updated decision

The cadence sweep answers the central question clearly:

- the weekly winner is not purely "weekly-only"
- but it is also not daily-robust
- the profitable zone is currently multi-day swing holding
- the likely engineering bridge is:
  - `every_5 -> every_4 -> every_3 -> every_2`

So the next intraday design work should be treated as:

- a controlled compression of a multi-day holding strategy
- not as proof that we already have a same-day intraday tradable system

### Is weekly rebalance still intraday?

No.

Weekly rebalance here means:

- entries are decided at a session open
- positions are then held across multiple trading days until the next weekly rebalance point

That is a short-term swing strategy, not same-day intraday flat trading.

### Can the same annualized return be claimed under strict same-day intraday-only trading?

No.

Current evidence does not support that.

In fact, today’s sweep showed:

- daily rebalancing versions were negative
- weekly multi-day holding versions were positive

So the present edge depends on holding across days. We should not claim the same CAGR for strict same-day entry-exit trading.
